TEMPO

Privacy policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026

In short: TEMPO has no accounts. Your progress stays on your phone. We do not sell your data.

The free app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collect some device data. Anonymous gameplay statistics are only sent if you agree. Buying TEMPO Pro removes the ads.

Who we are

TEMPO is made by Nezr Kaan, an independent developer in the United Kingdom. We are the data controller for the data described here. Contact: nezrkaan.business@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

Your ratings, puzzle history, records, streaks, missions, badges, settings and the nickname you choose for challenges are stored on your device only. In the store app a copy is also kept in the app's private storage, so your progress survives if the phone clears web data. We cannot see this data. To delete it, use Settings > reset rating & stats / reset records, or uninstall the app. Settings > export my data gives you a copy as a file.

Anonymous gameplay statistics (optional)

If, and only if, you agree (you are asked on first launch and can change it any time in Settings > share anonymous gameplay data), TEMPO sends events such as "a 1:00 run ended with this score" or "this puzzle was solved in 4 seconds" to PostHog (servers in the EU), together with a random install id, the app version and the platform. No name, email, contacts or location are sent. We use this only to improve the game. To have these events deleted, email us the install id shown in your exported data. Statistics are kept for up to two years.

Ads (free version of the store app)

The free app shows ads from Google AdMob between games and as optional rewarded videos (for an extra life, a streak freeze or a streak repair). Google may collect your device's advertising identifier (on iPhone only if you allow tracking), your IP address and a coarse location derived from it, device and app information, ad interactions and diagnostics, to show ads, measure them and prevent fraud.

  • In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland you are asked for consent through Google's consent form; you can change your choice in Settings > ad privacy choices.
  • On iPhone you are asked whether TEMPO may track you (App Tracking Transparency). If you say no, you still see ads, just not personalised with your advertising identifier.
  • How Google uses this data: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.
  • TEMPO Pro removes all ads. The website version has no ads.

Purchases

TEMPO Pro is sold through the App Store and Google Play. Apple and Google process the payment; we never see your card or bank details. To check which purchases are active, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous app user id, your purchase history for TEMPO and basic device information (revenuecat.com/privacy).

Puzzles from your own games

When you type a Lichess or Chess.com username and tap scan, your device asks lichess.org or api.chess.com directly for that player's recent public games, and downloads the chess engine (Stockfish) from cdn.jsdelivr.net. These services see your IP address, and Lichess / Chess.com see the username you typed; their own privacy policies apply. The games are analysed on your device and the results stay there. We do not receive the username or the games.

Challenge links and sharing

A challenge link contains the nickname you chose, your score, the mode and which puzzles you played. It only goes where you share it. Opening a link plays the same puzzles on the TEMPO website or app; nothing is stored on a server of ours.

Reminders

If you turn on daily reminders, they are scheduled on your device as local notifications. There is no push server. Turn them off in Settings or in your phone's settings.

Children

TEMPO is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Your rights

You can ask us for access to, correction or deletion of personal data we hold, object to processing, or withdraw consent at any time (this does not affect earlier processing). Most data never leaves your device, so in practice you control it yourself. You can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk) or your local data protection authority.
